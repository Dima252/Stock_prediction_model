Why it is quiet

This monitor is silent most of the time

The strategy waits for the whole market to sell off, not for one stock to look cheap. That condition has held on roughly 17% of trading days over 27 years. On the other 83%, there is nothing to say, and saying nothing is the correct output.

If you find yourself checking daily hoping for a signal, that is the failure mode this page is built to resist.

The rule

Four conditions, no model

There is no machine-learning model behind this page. A model was built, tested, and discarded because it did not survive an honest out-of-sample test. What remains is four lines:

dist_sma200_atr > 0        # the stock's long-term uptrend is intact
oversold_breadth > 0.20    # the whole market is oversold, not just this name
resid_ret5_atr > -0.5      # the fall is NOT specific to this company
rsi2 < 10                  # a sharp short-term washout

Entry at the next open. Exit when the close recovers above its 5-day average, or a stop one ATR below entry, or ten sessions — whichever comes first. Typical holding period is 2.2 sessions.

Why market-wide, not stock-specific

The third condition is the one people find surprising. The obvious idea is that a stock falling on its own should bounce hardest. That was tested first, and it was negative across all twelve exit schemes.

The reason it fails, in hindsight: a stock falling alone is falling for a company-specific reason, and that reason does not resolve itself in five days. A stock falling because everything is falling has no such reason attached to it. Inverting the failed hypothesis produced the best rule in the study.

Why the exit is a signal, not a target

Twelve exit schemes were compared. Ranked by return per trade, the widest bracket won — but it held positions four times longer. Ranked by return per day of capital committed, the ordering completely inverted:

Method

How it was tested

Financial backtests fail in specific, well-documented ways. Four defences were built before any result was believed.

1. The harness was tested on pure noise first

Before testing any strategy, the validation code was fed random numbers with no signal in them at all. A correct harness must find nothing.

An ordinary cross-validation invents an AUC of 0.561 and +0.65R per trade out of pure noise — squarely inside the range people report as a discovered edge. The cause is overlapping labels: trades that share calendar time land on both sides of the split.

2. Confidence intervals are computed over months, not trades

Trades here are heavily clustered — a single oversold day can generate 180 positions that all share one market bounce. Treating those as 180 independent observations produces an interval roughly four times too narrow. Every interval on this site resamples whole calendar blocks instead.

3. Everything is compared against the same day

The benchmark is not "the market" or "all trades" — it is a set of randomly chosen liquid stocks bought on the same day. This removes the market, the regime and the calendar in one step. It matters: an earlier version of this study measured against a pooled benchmark and overstated its result by roughly 15×, because the model was concentrating its picks into favourable months and being scored for it.

4. The final test period was used once

All decisions — features, setups, exits, model — were made on data up to 2020. The 2021–2025 period was scored once, at the end. A test set you tune against is not a test set.

What did not survive

Ten mean-reversion setups were tested. Four cleared zero in development; one cleared it out of sample. That ratio is roughly what chance produces among four marginal candidates — which is exactly why the holdout exists.

The famous breakout systems lost money

Minervini's volatility-contraction breakout, O'Neil's CANSLIM pivot and Darvas boxes were all implemented with the trend templates, relative-strength ranking, base-depth and market-regime filters their authors specify, and given a 60-day trailing stop with no profit target so winners could run.

All three underperformed buying random liquid stocks and trailing them, by 0.16 to 0.27R per trade, in development and out of sample. The one trend entry that did work was Weinstein's stage-2 — an early entry as price clears a rising 30-week average, not an extended entry at a breakout pivot.

Status

Live forward test

The rule is being paper-traded forward, logged automatically each day. No orders are placed. This is the only test that nothing has contaminated.

Limits

Read this before believing any of it

  • The edge is not confirmed. The forward test is the real test and it has nowhere near enough observations yet.
  • The holdout was read more than once across the project's iterations. The rule was selected on development data before it was read, which protects it, but the published interval does not price in the full search.
  • Survivorship bias is present and unfixed. The universe is current index membership, so companies that were delisted are missing. This inflates results, including the benchmark.
  • Positive in only 59% of years. 2002, 2013 and 2018 were clearly negative. A bad year is normal, not evidence of breakage.
  • Costs. The edge is robust to slippage because the benchmark pays it too, but raw returns go negative beyond roughly 35bps round trip.

Not investment advice. This is a research tool documenting a statistical study. It reports what has happened historically under specific conditions; it does not predict what will happen, and the edge it describes is unconfirmed.
